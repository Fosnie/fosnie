# Checking who is calling

## changelog

Added a list of names a telephone line checks callers against before offering them anything, enforced so that no caller is put through to a person until they have been checked and found clear, with the result recorded against the call and never disclosed to the caller.

## site

A line can now check a caller against your own records before it offers them anything. Keep a list of the names that matter, and a caller who matches has a message taken instead of being put through, and is told only that somebody will be in touch. The check is enforced rather than requested: while you keep a list, nobody reaches a person until they have been checked.

## detail

Any practice that has to think about conflicts has the same problem with an answering service: by the time somebody realises who is on the telephone, they have already been offered something. This is the check that comes first.

Keep a list of names against the account a line runs as: clients, matters, the other side of a matter, anybody you need to know about before offering an appointment. Paste it as it comes out of your own system, one per line, with a note saying which matter if that helps. Spelling, punctuation, titles, company endings and word order are all ignored when a caller is checked, so "Mrs Jane Fraser" is found under "Fraser, Jane" and "Acme" is found under "Acme Limited". A middle name given on the telephone and missing from your list does not stop a match, and a shared surname does not cause one.

The answer is one of three things, and only one of them lets a call go further. Clear means nothing on your list resembles the caller. A match means a person has to handle it. And a caller who will give only a surname is neither: one word identifies nobody, so it counts as not checked rather than as cleared.

**The check is enforced, not requested.** While an account keeps a list, a call on its lines cannot be put through to anybody until it has been checked and found clear. That includes never having been checked at all, so an agent that forgets to check refuses the transfer rather than making one, and a caller who will not give their full name has a message taken instead. An account with no list is unaffected, and its lines behave exactly as they did before.

**The list is never read out, and the caller is never told a check happened.** The agent is given a decision and an instruction, never a name, never how close a match was, and never anything it could repeat as evidence that a list exists. This matters more than it might appear: saying "you are on our records" tells the caller that the practice is involved in a matter concerning them, and the caller may be the other side of it. So a matched caller hears that a message has been taken and that somebody will be in touch, which is true, and nothing else.

The check runs against a list held for the purpose rather than against your document Libraries, and that is deliberate. Anything attached to the answering agent as a Library is read into the conversation, which would put your client list in front of an anonymous caller; and searching documents returns passages rather than a yes or a no, so it could not answer the question even if that were safe. Comparing a name against a list is a matter of looking, and it happens instantly and identically every time.

Who may see what follows the same rule as the rest of this feature. The account the list belongs to may read and change it, and so may an administrator of the platform. Somebody who may register telephone numbers can see that a list exists and how many names are on it, and cannot read them: registering a line and reading a practice's client list are not the same permission. Every call's result is recorded against the call for whoever triages it, and each check is written to the audit trail with the name the caller gave, never with the name it resembled.

Two things worth knowing. Nothing on a telephone verifies who anybody is, so a caller who gives a false name is checked against the name they gave: this check narrows a risk rather than removing one. And a false match costs a message instead of a transfer, which is the right way round for a check of this kind, so keep the note against each name useful enough to resolve one quickly.
